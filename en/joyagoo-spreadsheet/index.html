01 / The starting point

What Is a Joyagoo Spreadsheet?

A Joyagoo spreadsheet is usually a curated collection of product links, categories, and finds put together by community members or creators.

Its value is not the spreadsheet format itself. Its value is that it turns scattered links from sources like Weidian, Taobao, and 1688 into a simpler place to start.

It pulls the question of what to browse today out of messy feeds and into a more organized starting point.

Think of a useful sheet as a research notebook. One row might hold a pair of shoes you noticed yesterday; another might record a bag you want to compare later. The rows help you return to an idea without retracing every social post, saved image, or message that led you there.

A helpful entry gives you enough context to recognize the item again: a descriptive name, the source link, a category, and a short note explaining why you saved it. If you record an option or a price reference, add the date you checked it. That makes it easier to distinguish your earlier notes from information you still need to confirm.

A collection can reflect its creator's taste, so treat the selection as a starting point for your own choices. Being included in a sheet does not establish how an item fits, how it feels, or whether its listing is still available. Those are separate questions to carry into the next stage of your research.

02 / What works well

What a Spreadsheet Helps With

Finding a first direction

It gives shoppers a first-pass filter by shoes, accessories, beauty, electronics, clothing, or a specific style direction before they open deeper pages.

This is useful when your idea is still broad. You may know you want something for everyday wear without knowing whether to start with footwear, a layer, or a small accessory. A quick category scan lets you choose one direction before spending time on individual listings.

Collecting and scanning links in one place

Saving a link is a small decision; choosing an item takes more thought. A sheet gives those two activities separate space. You can collect a few possibilities now, then return when you have time to compare them. Add a brief reason for saving each one so the list remains useful after the original excitement has passed.

Organizing scattered finds from multiple sources

Use a consistent naming system even when the original listings look different. A plain description such as “black shoulder bag, short strap” is easier to recognize than an unexplained link. Keep the original source alongside your description so you can revisit the listing and check the exact option you meant to save.

Keep only the notes that support a decision

You do not need a complicated tracker to get value from a spreadsheet. Start with the category, item name, source URL, selected option, and one open question. A final column for your next action can separate an interesting idea from something you are ready to investigate. Useful actions include comparing dimensions, checking a size chart, or removing a duplicate.

For example, two saved bags might serve different purposes even if both are black. One may interest you as a compact evening option, while the other needs to fit your everyday essentials. Recording the intended use helps you compare each bag against the right requirement instead of choosing only by its thumbnail.

03 / When to switch views

Where a Spreadsheet Slows Down

A spreadsheet is useful when you want one place to collect links. It gets weaker when you need clearer comparisons, category decisions, and a faster next step.

A spreadsheet is good for organizing links, but weak for the visual question of what really separates one option from another.

As more links pile up, it becomes harder to tell at a glance which ones are actually worth opening.

A product link can show the item itself, but it rarely answers the question of how it may actually feel once worn.

Rows do not always show the differences that matter

Two shoe entries can share a color and a broad style while leaving you with different questions about shape, sizing, and intended use. A short title cannot answer all of those questions. At that point, open a smaller group of relevant options and compare the same details for each one. A focused shoes page gives you a more manageable place to begin that comparison.

More saved links can mean more unfinished decisions

If you keep reopening the same rows without learning anything new, pause the collection stage. Choose a few candidates and write down what would make one preferable. Perhaps you need a particular dimension, a different shape, or an option that suits clothes you already own. Removing an unsuitable item is useful progress, even when you have not chosen a replacement.

Keep uncertainty visible. A blank measurement field means you still need a measurement; it should not quietly become an assumption that the item will fit. The same applies to materials, available options, and details missing from photographs. Marking the unanswered question helps you decide what to check instead of letting a polished image make the decision.

Try reading your shortlist without opening any links. Can you explain why each item is there and what you still need to learn? If several rows have the same vague note, rewrite them around the differences you care about. If you cannot remember why you saved a link, move it out of the active shortlist. This small edit keeps old ideas available without asking you to reconsider them every time you browse.

A picture is only part of the comparison

Photographs can help you examine visible details, but they do not let you try something on. For clothing, compare the listed measurements with a garment you already like wearing. For a bag, consider the dimensions and how you plan to carry it. If the information you need is missing, keep the item under consideration rather than treating the gap as resolved.

Choose the page that matches your unresolved question

If your question is “Which kind of item do I want?”, stay with category browsing. If it is “Which of these few options suits my needs?”, narrow your shortlist and compare the same characteristics. If it is “What should I check before continuing?”, move to the buying guide. You do not have to complete every page in a fixed order.

For bags, jewelry, and finishing details, start with accessories and decide which practical details matter to you. A bag comparison might start with capacity and strap length. A jewelry comparison might start with dimensions and fastening. Keeping one type of item in view makes it easier to ask consistent questions across your saved options.

You can also return to the sheet after using a guide. Add the detail you learned, change the next action, and keep the original link attached to that note. The aim is to make each visit answer a question. You might finish with one promising option, or discover that none of your saved items fits the purpose. Either result is more useful than adding another row without knowing what it contributes.

A simple way to leave the spreadsheet with a useful shortlist

  1. Choose one purpose. Write down what you need the item for. “A compact bag for evenings” gives you a clearer comparison than “something new.”
  2. Keep a small set of candidates. Remove duplicates and links that no longer match your purpose. Leave enough choice to compare without recreating the entire sheet.
  3. Record the missing details. Give each candidate one concrete next check, such as dimensions, sizing information, or a closer look at the selected option.
  4. Continue when the next step is clear. Use the relevant category or buying guide, then return to your notes only when you have something useful to add.

Keep your saved reference beside you so you can compare the exact item and option before proceeding. A tidy shortlist is useful preparation; the actual buying process happens outside this guide.

05 / A few final questions

Common Questions

What should I open after the spreadsheet?

Usually a tighter category page such as shoes or accessories, or a buying guide if you are closer to checks, links, and the next official step.

Can I skip the spreadsheet and go to a category page directly?

Yes. Once the rough direction is clear, category pages are usually easier to scan than more rows.

Does the spreadsheet replace the official Joyagoo site?

No. This site helps with discovery and browsing. Joyagoo handles your account, orders, payment, warehouse QC, and shipping.
